Strategic Snapshot

Last week, someone from one of Canada's largest asset managers asked me about AI training for their advisors.

My first instinct was to say yes—build curriculum, teach prompts, show tools. But something stopped me.

Because here's what I've learned: You don't learn AI. You learn to use AI.

The difference isn't semantic—it's everything. Learning AI means understanding how models work, what tokens are, how training data shapes outputs. That's interesting. But it's not what creates business value.

Learning to use AI means having the frameworks—the lenses—that determine what AI can see and do for you.

Without frameworks, AI gives you generic output. With frameworks, AI becomes extension of your strategic thinking.

Here's what changed my thinking: In the AI age, the most valuable skill isn't prompting—it's creating the lenses that determine what's possible to see.

Think about it like a camera. You can have the best camera in the world, but without choosing your lens—wide angle, telephoto, macro—you're stuck with whatever default view the camera gives you.

Frameworks are your lenses. They focus the aperture. They determine what gets magnified, what gets revealed, what patterns become visible.

And here's the generative part: Once you create a lens, it doesn't just organize information—it expands what you can see everywhere. You point it at a fund pitch, suddenly four dimensions appear. You point it at your own messaging, the same dimensions reveal themselves. The lens makes patterns visible that were always there but impossible to see without it.

🔍 Framework Spotlight: The Rhetoric Lens

A lens for understanding why messages work—and creating messages that land

Every compelling message—whether it's a fund pitch, your value proposition, or an email to a client—needs four dimensions working together. Miss one, and your message falls flat.

🎖️ ETHOS — Credibility
Why should anyone believe you? Your proof, track record, credentials. The reasons someone should trust what you're saying. 		❤️ PATHOS — Emotion
Why should anyone care? The feeling, the problem you solve, the desire you fulfill. The human element that makes someone lean in.
⏰ KAIROS — Timing
Why now? The relevance, context, reason this matters RIGHT NOW in this moment, not six months from now. 		📊 LOGOS — Logic
What are the facts? The rational case, the data, the metrics. The logical reasons that justify the decision.

The Lens in Action:

Use it to learn: "Why does this fund pitch work so well? Let me analyze it through the Rhetoric lens..."

Use it to create: "I need to build a compelling message. Let me ensure I have all four dimensions..."

Once you have this lens, you start seeing these four dimensions everywhere. In advisor messaging. In marketing copy. In political speeches. The lens doesn't just organize—it reveals patterns you couldn't see before.

🛠️ Tool of the Month: Framework-Powered Analysis

Use Class: Internal Only

Time Investment: 10 minutes

What it solves: Generic AI output that doesn't match your thinking

Step 1: Pick Something You Want to Understand Better

A fund pitch that landed perfectly. A competitor's messaging that resonates. Your own best client email.

Copy the text. You're going to use the Rhetoric lens to SEE why it works.

Step 2: Give AI the Lens

Paste this into ChatGPT or Claude:

Analyze this message using the Rhetoric Framework: ETHOS (Credibility): What makes this trustworthy? What proof is provided? PATHOS (Emotion): What feeling does this create? What problem/desire does it address? KAIROS (Timing): Why does this feel relevant right now? What context makes this timely? LOGOS (Logic): What facts, data, or rational arguments support this? For each dimension, show me: 1. What's present (quote specific phrases) 2. What's missing or weak 3. How the four dimensions work together Here's the message: [paste your text]

Step 3: Now Flip It—Create Instead of Analyze

Use the same lens to BUILD a message. Try this prompt:

I need to write [client email / LinkedIn post / fund commentary]. Help me build it using the Rhetoric Framework. Ask me questions to develop each dimension: ETHOS, PATHOS, KAIROS, LOGOS. Then draft something that integrates all four.

What You'll Notice:

AI without a lens: Generic, vague, sounds like everyone else

AI with a lens: Specific, strategic, reveals patterns you'd miss

💡 Pro tip: Save the Rhetoric Framework as a custom GPT or prompt template. Every time you analyze or create messaging, run it through the lens. The pattern recognition compounds.
